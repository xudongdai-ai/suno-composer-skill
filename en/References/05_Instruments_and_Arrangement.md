# Instruments and Arrangement

**Use this category to assign roles and plan entrances, exits, register and density.**

## Five layers

| Layer | Function | Useful strategies | Common conflict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead | Theme or foreground | Voice, cello solo, lead synth, guzheng | Too many competing lead colors |
| Counterline | Answer or independent line | Viola answer, flute echo, guitar countermelody | Same rhythm/register masking the lead |
| Harmony | Middle-voice support | Rhodes thirds/sevenths, strings, horns | Muddy low mids and duplicated roots |
| Bass | Pitch direction and foundation | Walking bass, sub, cello/bassoon pedal | Competition with kick or low brass |
| Pulse / texture | Time and space | Drums, pizzicato, brushes, atmosphere | Constant high density with no contrast |

## Orchestral colors and motion

- **Strings:** separate high violin legato from a cello's melodic register. Divisi can create a chord bed; spiccato creates pulse; sul ponticello tremolo adds instability.
- **Woodwinds:** flute air, oboe presence, clarinet warmth and bassoon depth can alternate as answers instead of always doubling the lead.
- **Brass:** horn can carry a rounded theme, trombone adds low weight and trumpet a bright call. Stopped horn can add tension. Full brass needs room in the arrangement.
- **Percussion:** use timpani for structural accents, bass drum for selected arrivals and cymbal swells for transitions. Every climax does not require trailer impacts.
- **Harp / keyboards:** glissandi, resonance, celesta points and prepared-piano color can support a specific role. Treat unusual techniques as intentional choices.

## Counterpoint and its limits

For two independent lines, use complementary rhythm and contrasting motion; review parallel fifths/octaves when aiming for traditional practice. A fugue plan needs an identifiable subject, a deliberate answer register/key, a contrasting countersubject and developing episodes. Controlled aleatoric textures describe a sound, not a verified readable score. Note-level checking requires notes.

## Modern rhythm-section roles

- Kick provides impact; bass provides pitch direction. Do not give both maximum low-frequency occupancy at every moment.
- Let one of guitar/keys carry syncopation while the other supplies open harmony or a counterline.
- Keep the main vocal forward, backing harmonies wider and ad-libs out of the hook's way.
- Separate mono-compatible sub, characterful mid bass, foreground lead and wider pad; check masking.

## Density arcs

- **Film:** sparse piano 1/5 → cello and viola 2/5 → string pulse 3/5 → brass/percussion peak 5/5 → solo reprise 1/5.
- **Pop:** contained verse 2/5 → growing pre-chorus 3/5 → broad chorus 5/5 → stripped bridge 1/5 → final chorus 5/5.
- **Techno:** groove 2/5 → hats 3/5 → kick subtraction 1/5 → bass return 4/5 → filtered outro 2/5.

Density numbers describe a plan, not a Suno control setting.

## Useful English descriptions

- `The solo cello states a descending three-note motif, answered by muted violas in contrary motion.`
- `A restrained bass clarinet countermelody enters only on the second phrase.`
- `The bass carries the functional roots while the Rhodes voices mostly thirds, sevenths, and ninths.`
- `High strings sustain divisi harmonics; lower strings articulate an understated spiccato ostinato.`
- `Drop the percussion before the reprise; return with a broader register, not a louder wall.`

## Listening check

Can you hear the lead, the answer, changes in density and distinct bass/drum roles? If not, simplify the prompt or rebuild the arrangement in an editable production workflow. The written plan alone does not establish an audible result.
