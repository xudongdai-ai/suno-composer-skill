# Regional and Cross-Cultural

Part of the 81-profile library. Choose one primary genre, then add a secondary influence only when it has a clear role.

These are adaptable writing prototypes. Regional, historical and stylistic variants may differ. Apply harmony to the actual mode and melody. Create audible section contrast through changing instrumentation, inversions, counterpoint or drum intensity. Original Styles examples describe targets; they do not lock chords, duration or structure.

## 01. Chinese-inspired guofeng pop (`regional-guofeng`)

- **Tempo / Metre**: 70–120 BPM; 4/4 or 6/8.

- **Harmony and tonal vocabulary**: pentatonic motif over pop diatonic/borrowed iv palette

- **Form**: Intro–Verse–Pre–Chorus–Instrumental Solo–Final Chorus

- **Groove**: modern pop backbeat with restrained traditional percussion.

- **Lead instruments and solo roles**: guzheng or erhu motifs, Mandarin vocal.

- **Supporting instruments and rhythm layers**: dizi flourishes, strings, piano, bass.

- **Production and space**: balanced acoustic-urban hybrid, not ornamental overload.

- **Avoid and boundaries**: avoid treating pentatonic color as a fixed historical genre.

**Original Suno Styles example** — shorten to fit your current field budget; adapt to the actual work.

```text
Chinese-inspired guofeng pop, 70–120 BPM target, 4/4 or 6/8 feel, modern pop backbeat with restrained traditional percussion; lead: guzheng or erhu motifs, Mandarin vocal; supporting layers: dizi flourishes, strings, piano, bass; harmonic color: pentatonic motif over pop diatonic/borrowed iv palette; structure evolving from Intro to Verse to Pre to Chorus to Instrumental Solo to Final Chorus; production: balanced acoustic-urban hybrid, not ornamental overload.
```
